# 0004. Position the app as mind training, never as treatment

- Status: Accepted
- Date: 2026-10-07

## Context
Brain-training apps have been fined for claiming to prevent or delay dementia (FTC v. Lumosity, 2016). Medical claims would also make the app a regulated medical device.

## Decision
The wording is "mind training" and "keep your mind active". The app never claims to prevent, slow or treat Alzheimer's disease or brain injury, and it shows no diagnostic score. Onboarding tells users to see a doctor if they are worried about memory. Pill Box shows "This is practice, not medical advice" and uses made-up pills. Validated tasks (e.g. m2c2kit) may only be added as progress tracking.

## Consequences
- Lower regulatory risk. The evidence (docs/RESEARCH-AND-DESIGN.md) still guides the design.
- Licensed clinical instruments (MoCA, ACE-III, Boston Naming Test) are out of scope.
